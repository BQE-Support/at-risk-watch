Methodology & scope notes

Data sources

Two systems feed this page. Salesforce supplies account attributes and its own packaged health score. Zendesk supplies support-ticket risk signals from 7 saved "Risk" views. The two are joined on the Salesforce Account Id, which Zendesk stores against each Organization record via its native Salesforce integration — the same link the account team's existing support tooling relies on.

Segments

  • Scope: Salesforce Accounts, Type = Customer, synced to Zendesk, and either ARR ≤ $20,000 or CSM Owned = true (any ARR)
  • Main ($8k–$20k): ARR between $8,000 and $20,000
  • Main (Premium, <$8k): ARR under $8,000 but a Premium Support customer — kept with the main group rather than the low-ARR segment
  • Low ARR (<$8k, non-Premium): ARR under $8,000 and not Premium Support
  • CSM Owned (>$20k): ARR over $20,000 — at BQE, crossing $20k ARR is what gets an account a dedicated CSM, so this segment is effectively the CSM-managed population. Added 2026-08-18; previously excluded entirely by a hard ARR≤$20k ceiling on the underlying data pull, so no toggle could ever surface these accounts (e.g. MC Consultants, Inc. at $50,107 ARR).

At-risk criteria (segment-specific, since 2026-07-28)

  • Main ($8k–$20k), Main (Premium, <$8k) & CSM Owned (>$20k): 1 or more "clean flags" — an NPS detractor response, zero Subscription Usage score, a flat/negative Pendo usage trend, or zero Invoicing Usage score. These four are direct engagement/satisfaction measurements, not ticket-count ratios.
  • Churn intent override (all segments, since 2026-11-24): a ticket in the last 60 days where the customer said they may cancel, not renew or switch makes the account at risk (Critical) whatever its Salesforce health or segment, and it is shown even when the Low ARR and CSM-owned toggles are off. A stated intent to leave is a different kind of signal from an aged ticket, which is why it can flag an account on its own.
  • Negative sentiment (supporting, since 2026-11-24): 2 or more tickets Zendesk's AI rated negative in 30 days adds a reason and raises severity, and counts as one Zendesk signal for Low ARR. It never flags Main, Premium or CSM accounts by itself: it reads tone, so it also marks frustrated or urgent customers who are not leaving.
  • Low ARR (<$8k, non-Premium): still the prior criteria (Salesforce Health Status = "At Risk" OR 2+ Zendesk signals) — see caveat below on why, and why this segment is excluded by default.

Replaced the single portfolio-wide rule (Health Status = "At Risk" OR 2+ Zendesk signals) after finding it was ~98% driven by the Health Status label alone, which flags 55% of Low ARR accounts regardless of support activity — producing a 1,440-account list nobody could act on. Ticket Volume and Escalation Rate scores were also found to be unreliable at small accounts (a single ticket can swing them from 0 to 100%), so the new criteria deliberately avoids them. CSM Owned (>$20k) uses the same bar as Main/Premium by default since it hasn't been tuned separately yet — worth revisiting once there's feedback on how well it fits a CSM-managed population.

Known caveats

  • Low ARR and CSM Owned are excluded from every view by default (KPIs, chart, table) via the "Include Low ARR" / "Include CSM-owned" toggles. Tightening Low ARR's criteria the same way as Main/Premium hit a cliff, not a gradient (2+ clean flags = 839 accounts, 3+ = 17, nothing usable between) — tiny accounts score low on every usage dimension as a baseline characteristic, which an absolute threshold can't distinguish from real decline. Needs trend data, not a different cutoff. Toggle either on to see the segment anyway.
  • One account carries an unreadable health sub-score in Salesforce and is shown with a blank Health value
